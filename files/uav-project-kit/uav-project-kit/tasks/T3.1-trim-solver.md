## Background

A plane in steady cruise is perfectly balanced: lift cancels weight, thrust
cancels drag, nothing is rotating. There is one exact combination of nose angle,
elevator and throttle that achieves this at a given airspeed. Finding it is
called trimming.

You do not solve it algebraically — the equations are nonlinear and coupled.
You let the computer hunt: guess, measure how unbalanced the aircraft still is,
adjust, repeat. Standard root-finding.

Trim is the foundation of everything in Phase 4 onward. Controllers are designed
around trim points; linearisation happens at trim points; every simulation starts
at one. A trim solver that converges to a slightly wrong answer will make every
controller look slightly bad and you will never know why.

Good initial guesses matter more than the solver choice:
`alpha_0 = 2 * m * g / (rho * V**2 * S * C_L_alpha)`, `delta_e_0 = 0`,
`delta_t_0 = 0.5`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Converges at all 9 grid points `V ∈ {18, 25, 35}` × `gamma ∈ {-5°, 0°, +5°}`
- [ ] Residual `< 1e-10` at every point
- [ ] **Physically sensible:** as `V` decreases, `alpha` must increase (you need
      more angle of attack to hold the same weight at lower speed). Assert
      monotonicity across the grid
- [ ] As `gamma` increases, `delta_t` must increase (climbing needs power)
- [ ] Simulating from a trim point with the trim controls held constant, the
      aircraft holds altitude within 1 m and airspeed within 0.1 m/s over 60 s.
      **This is the real test** — it confirms the trim point actually trims
- [ ] `beta = 0`, `phi = 0`, `p = q = r = 0` at every trim point
- [ ] Trimming outside the envelope (e.g. `V = 5`) raises `TrimNotConverged`
      with a useful message, rather than returning a garbage answer

## Notes

The 60-second hold test is the one that matters. A solver can report a tiny
residual and still be trimming the wrong thing — for example if you forgot to
include `qdot` in the residual vector, or if `theta = alpha + gamma` was applied
with the wrong sign. Only flying it proves it.

If trim converges at level flight but fails in climb, suspect propulsion (T2.5)
before suspecting the solver.
