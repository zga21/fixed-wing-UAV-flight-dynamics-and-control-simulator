## Background

This task produces the deliverable that answers RQ4, and it is the most
genuinely novel output of the project.

The GP knows where it is uncertain. Sweep it across the flight envelope, plot
that uncertainty, and you get a **map of where your model is trustworthy** —
regions where the aerodynamic model is well supported by data, and regions where
it is guessing.

That is a real engineering artefact. It says: "the controller has been validated
here, is expected to work here, and enters unmodelled territory here." Very few
student or portfolio projects produce anything like it.

But it is only meaningful if the uncertainty estimate is *honest*, which is what
calibration checking establishes first.

Overlaying the training-point density is the detail that makes the figure
persuasive: low confidence with no nearby data is expected and reassuring; low
confidence *with* dense nearby data means the residual is genuinely hard to
model, which is a different and more interesting statement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **Calibrated.** 95% predictive interval achieves 92–98% empirical coverage
      on the held-out set. Outside that range the GP is over- or under-confident
      and the map is not trustworthy — refit before continuing
- [ ] Reliability diagram plotted and roughly on the diagonal
- [ ] Confidence map covers the full envelope with training density overlaid
- [ ] **Low-confidence regions identified and explained in words.** Typically
      high alpha near stall and the low-airspeed corner. If a region is
      unexpectedly uncertain, investigate — it may indicate a modelling problem
      rather than a data gap
- [ ] The map is cross-referenced against the Phase 6 mismatch crossover points:
      do the regions where the model is uncertain coincide with the regions where
      NDI degrades? If so, that is a strong, coherent story linking RQ2 and RQ4
- [ ] `docs/uncertainty_findings.md` states the conclusion in two paragraphs
- [ ] The figure is publication quality — labelled axes, units, colourbar, and a
      caption that stands alone

## Notes

This figure is likely to be the centrepiece of the website and one of the two or
three figures a reader remembers. Spend the extra hour on it.

The cross-reference with Phase 6 is what turns two separate results into a single
argument: *the model is least certain here, and that is precisely where the
model-based controller loses its advantage.* If your data supports that, it is the
best sentence in the report.
