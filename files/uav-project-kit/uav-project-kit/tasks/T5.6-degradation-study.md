## Background

This is the first real research result of the project, and it is worth treating
as such rather than as a debugging exercise.

You have a controller that works perfectly on a perfect aircraft. You now have
four separate mechanisms of realism. Turning them on one at a time tells you
**which one actually matters** — and that is a genuinely useful engineering
finding, not just a number.

It also directly answers RQ1: how much does nominal controller performance
deteriorate when the environment and hardware are realistic?

## Notes

Expect the realistic configuration to be substantially worse — that is the
point, and it is what motivates the entire rest of the project. If C5 is barely
worse than C0, something is not connected: check T5.4 first, then confirm the
controller is reading `x_hat` from the EKF rather than the true state.

This table is one of the more quotable results you will produce. "Actuator rate
limiting alone accounts for 60% of the total degradation" is a concrete, specific
engineering finding.
