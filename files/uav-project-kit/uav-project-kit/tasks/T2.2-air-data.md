## Background

Aerodynamic forces do not depend on how fast the aircraft is moving over the
ground. They depend on how fast it is moving **through the air**, and at what
angle it meets the air.

Three numbers capture this:
- **V**, the true airspeed — the magnitude of the velocity relative to the air
- **alpha**, the angle of attack — how much the nose is pitched up relative to
  where the aircraft is actually going. This is the single most important
  variable in flight dynamics: lift is roughly proportional to it
- **beta**, the sideslip angle — how much the aircraft is flying sideways

Right now, with no wind, air-relative velocity equals body velocity. In Phase 5
wind is introduced and this function starts subtracting it. Structuring it now
so that wind slots in later, without touching the aerodynamics, is the point of
making this its own task.

## What to implement

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class AirData:
    V: float          # true airspeed, m/s
    alpha: float      # angle of attack, rad
    beta: float       # sideslip angle, rad
    q_bar: float      # dynamic pressure, 0.5 * rho * V^2, Pa
    v_air_b: np.ndarray   # (3,) air-relative velocity, body frame

def air_relative_velocity(vel_b: np.ndarray,
                          quat: np.ndarray,
                          wind_n: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Velocity of the aircraft relative to the air mass, in body axes.

        v_air_b = v_b - R_nb^T @ wind_n

    In Phase 2, wind_n is always zeros. In Phase 5 it carries steady wind plus
    turbulence. Every aerodynamic calculation uses THIS, never vel_b directly.

    Args: vel_b (3,) body velocity; quat (4,); wind_n (3,) NED wind vector
    Returns: (3,) air-relative velocity in body frame
    """

def compute_air_data(vel_b, quat, altitude, wind_n=None) -> AirData:
    """
        V     = |v_air_b|
        alpha = arctan2(w_air, u_air)
        beta  = arcsin(v_air / V)
        q_bar = 0.5 * rho(altitude) * V^2

    Guards:
      - V below V_MIN_AERO (default 0.1 m/s): return alpha = beta = 0 and
        q_bar = 0 rather than dividing by zero
      - arcsin argument clipped to [-1, 1] against floating-point drift
    """
```

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Straight and level, `vel_b = [25, 0, 0]` → `alpha = 0`, `beta = 0`,
      `V = 25`
- [ ] `vel_b = [25, 0, 2]` (descending relative to the nose) → `alpha > 0`.
      Assert the sign explicitly. A positive `w` means the air is coming from
      below, which is positive angle of attack
- [ ] `vel_b = [25, 2, 0]` → `beta > 0` (air from the right)
- [ ] `V = 0` returns zeros and does not raise or produce NaN
- [ ] With `wind_n = [-10, 0, 0]` (a 10 m/s headwind from the north) and the
      aircraft flying north at 25 m/s ground speed, `V` must be 35 m/s.
      Assert this now, before wind exists, so the Phase 5 wiring is already
      verified
- [ ] Round trip: constructing `v_air_b` from `V, alpha, beta` and feeding it
      back recovers the same angles to 1e-12

## Notes

The wind sign is easy to get backwards. Convention: `wind_n` is the velocity
**of the air mass** in the NED frame. A north wind of 10 m/s — meaning air
moving *toward* the north — is `[10, 0, 0]`. Meteorologists usually name winds
by where they come *from*, which is the opposite. Write your choice into the
docstring and into `docs/conventions.md`.
