## Background

The coefficients from T2.3 are dimensionless numbers. This task turns them into
actual Newtons and Newton-metres, and — the part that trips people up — rotates
them into the right frame.

Lift and drag are defined in the **wind frame**: drag points backwards along the
relative wind, lift points perpendicular to it. But the equations of motion in
T1.3 need forces in the **body frame**. The rotation between them is by the angle
of attack. Skip it, or get its sign wrong, and the aircraft will fly but the
drag will be pointing slightly wrong, which quietly corrupts every trim solution
and every energy calculation.

Sideforce and all three moments are already in body axes and need no rotation.

Note the minus signs on `C_D` and `C_L` inside the matrix. Drag opposes motion
(so it is `-C_D` along `x_w`), and lift acts *upward*, which in a down-positive
body frame means a **negative** `F_z`. This is where sign errors hide.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At `alpha = 0`, `F_x_aero == -q_bar*S*C_D` and `F_z_aero == -q_bar*S*C_L`
      exactly. No rotation happens at zero alpha, so this is a clean check
- [ ] At `alpha = 90°`, lift and drag have swapped axes — assert the components
- [ ] **Lift acts upward.** In level flight with positive `C_L`, `F_z_aero` must
      be **negative** (up is `-z` in the body frame). This one line catches an
      inverted aircraft
- [ ] Magnitude sanity: at `V = 25`, sea level, `C_L = 0.4`, `S = 0.55`, lift is
      ~84 N — same order as the ~132 N weight. If lift comes out at 8 N or 8000 N,
      you have a units or `q_bar` error
- [ ] Doubling `V` quadruples all forces (they scale with `V²`)
- [ ] Symmetric flight produces exactly zero `F_y`, `M_x`, `M_z`
- [ ] Forces at `V = 0` are all zero, no NaN

## Notes

The order-of-magnitude check is worth doing by hand once, on paper, for your
actual aircraft. Write the arithmetic into the test docstring. It takes five
minutes and it is the check that catches a wing area in cm² or a density in
g/cm³ — the kind of error that no amount of unit testing of individual functions
will reveal.
