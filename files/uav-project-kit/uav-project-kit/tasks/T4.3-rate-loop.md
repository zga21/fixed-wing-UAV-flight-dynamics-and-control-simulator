## Background

The innermost and fastest loop. Its only job: "make the aircraft roll at 20
degrees per second" — and make it happen quickly and cleanly. It runs at 200 Hz.

Everything above it depends on this loop being fast and well-damped. If the rate
loop is sloppy, no amount of tuning in the outer loops will fix the aircraft,
because the outer loops assume their commands are followed promptly.

Three independent PIDs: roll rate to aileron, pitch rate to elevator, yaw rate to
rudder. They are treated as decoupled, which is an approximation — the Phase 3
decoupling check told you how good an approximation it is for your aircraft.

**Initial gain estimate from the linear model.** Do not tune by hand from
nothing. The roll subsidence pole from T3.3 gives you the plant time constant
`tau_roll` directly, so for a target closed-loop bandwidth `omega_target`:

```
Kp_roll ≈ omega_target * tau_roll / K_plant
```

where `K_plant` is the steady-state roll-rate-per-aileron gain from the `B`
matrix. Starting from a model-based estimate rather than trial and error is both
faster and more defensible in the report.

Target bandwidths: roll 15–25 rad/s, pitch 10–20 rad/s, yaw 5–10 rad/s.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Roll rate step of 30 °/s: rise time under 0.3 s, overshoot under 20%,
      steady-state error under 2%
- [ ] Pitch and yaw rate steps meet similar targets
- [ ] Closed-loop bandwidth measured (via a frequency sweep or from the step
      response) and recorded — T4.4 needs these numbers to set its own bandwidth
- [ ] No sustained oscillation over 30 s of held command
- [ ] Works at both `V = 18` and `V = 35` with the same gains. If it does not,
      that is your motivation for gain scheduling — record it, it belongs in the
      report and it is part of the answer to RQ2
- [ ] Control outputs stay inside limits

## Notes

Measure the bandwidth properly rather than eyeballing it — T4.4 and T4.5 both
depend on the 3–5x frequency separation rule, and that rule needs real numbers.
A simple approach: command a chirp, compute the frequency response by FFT of
input and output, find the −3 dB point.
