## Background

A small task with a large failure mode. The wind model exists; now every
aerodynamic calculation must use air-relative velocity rather than ground
velocity.

If any path still uses `vel_b`, wind will affect the aircraft's trajectory but
not its aerodynamics, which is physically incoherent: the aircraft would drift
with the wind but feel no change in lift or angle of attack. Turbulence would
have almost no effect on tracking, and your Phase 6 comparison would show the two
controllers performing suspiciously similarly.

You built T2.2 to accept a `wind_n` argument precisely so this task would be
small. Now confirm nothing bypasses it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **Steady wind changes airspeed.** Flying into a 10 m/s headwind at 25 m/s
      ground speed gives `V = 35` and correspondingly higher lift. Assert the
      lift change, not just the airspeed
- [ ] **Crosswind produces sideslip.** A pure crosswind with no control input
      produces non-zero `beta`
- [ ] **Turbulence perturbs alpha.** With turbulence on and controls frozen,
      `alpha` fluctuates and the aircraft's pitch response follows. If `alpha` is
      constant while the aircraft is being buffeted, wind is not reaching the
      aerodynamics
- [ ] Zero wind reproduces Phase 4 results **exactly**, bit for bit. This is the
      regression check that proves you changed nothing else
- [ ] The source-scanning test passes

## Notes

The zero-wind bit-identical check is the important one. It proves this task is a
pure addition rather than a silent modification of existing behaviour, which
means your Phase 4 baseline record is still valid and comparable.
