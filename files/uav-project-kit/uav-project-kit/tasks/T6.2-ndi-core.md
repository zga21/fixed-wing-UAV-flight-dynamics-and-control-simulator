## Background

The PID from Phase 4 works by noticing mistakes and correcting them. It has no
idea *why* the aircraft moves.

NDI is different. It carries a copy of the physics, so instead of reacting it
calculates: "I want the nose to rotate at this rate. My model says the aircraft
is currently producing that much moment by itself. So the elevator must supply
precisely the difference." It cancels the aircraft's own dynamics and replaces
them with the dynamics you asked for.

If the model were perfect, the closed loop would behave identically at 18 m/s and
40 m/s — one linear, speed-independent response across the whole envelope, with
no gain scheduling. That is the promise.

The catch, and the entire research question: the internal model is never exactly
right. How wrong can it be before NDI is worse than the PID it replaced?

## What to implement

```python
class NDIController(Controller):
    """
    Inner-loop NDI:
        nu    = K_omega * e_omega + K_I * integral(e_omega)
        delta = g(x)^-1 (nu - f(x))

    Reuses the Phase 4 attitude and outer loops unchanged, so the comparison
    isolates the inner loop -- which is where the nonlinearity lives and where
    the two approaches genuinely differ.
    """
    def __init__(self, model: ControlAffineModel, K_omega, K_I,
                 q_bar_min=5.0, cond_max=1e4, blend_V=14.0): ...

    def _virtual_control(self, omega_cmd, omega_hat, dt) -> np.ndarray: ...

    def _invert(self, nu, x_hat, air) -> np.ndarray:
        """
        Guards, in order:
          1. clamp q_bar to q_bar_min
          2. if cond(g) > cond_max, use a damped pseudo-inverse
          3. below blend_V, blend smoothly toward the PID baseline

        An unguarded inversion produces infinite commands at low airspeed and
        fills the simulation with NaN. The guards are not optional.
        """
```

**Why the integral term.** Pure NDI has no mechanism to reject steady modelling
error — if your `C_m_0` is off, the aircraft sits at a constant pitch offset
forever. The integral outside the inversion fixes that. It is also the thing that
makes NDI a fair competitor rather than a fragile textbook demo.

**Structural fairness.** Keep the Phase 4 attitude and outer loops. Changing
three layers at once and attributing the improvement to "the nonlinear
controller" would not be a defensible comparison.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **Perfect-model inversion.** With the controller model equal to the plant
      model, the closed-loop rate response matches the commanded first-order
      response `omega_dot = K_omega * (omega_cmd - omega)` to within 2%
- [ ] **Speed independence.** The same rate step response at `V = 18` and
      `V = 35` with no gain change. Overlay them and assert the difference is
      under 5%. This is the headline property, and the plot is a report figure
- [ ] **Low-speed guard.** At `V = 8` m/s the controller produces finite,
      in-limit commands. No NaN, no saturation spiral
- [ ] **Blending is smooth.** Sweep airspeed down through `blend_V` and confirm
      the control signal has no discontinuity
- [ ] **Steady error rejected.** With `C_m_0` in the controller model wrong by
      50%, the integral term drives steady-state pitch error to zero
- [ ] Implements the `Controller` protocol; `gains` exposes `K_omega` and `K_I`
- [ ] Deterministic

## Notes

Log `cond(g)` and whether each guard fired, into the diagnostics. In Phase 9 you
will want to know whether failures were control failures or numerical ones, and
having that flag in the results database is the difference between an afternoon
and a week of triage.

The speed-independence plot is the single most persuasive figure NDI gives you.
Two step responses at different airspeeds lying on top of each other, next to the
PID's two visibly different responses, makes the argument immediately.
