## Background

Air moves. Two kinds of movement matter: **steady wind**, which is a constant
drift the aircraft must crab into, and **turbulence**, which is random buffeting
across a spread of frequencies.

Turbulence is not white noise. Real atmospheric turbulence has a specific
frequency content — lots of energy at low frequencies (long, slow rolls of air)
and progressively less at high frequencies. The Dryden model captures this by
passing white noise through shaping filters whose transfer functions are chosen
so the output has the right spectrum.

This matters for controller comparison because turbulence energy near your
control bandwidth is what actually degrades tracking. White noise would put most
of its energy at frequencies the aircraft simply ignores, and would flatter your
controller.

## What to implement

```python
class SteadyWind:
    """Constant NED wind vector. Optionally with a shear profile in altitude."""
    def __init__(self, speed: float, direction_rad: float,
                 shear_exponent: float = 0.0): ...
    def velocity(self, altitude: float) -> np.ndarray: ...

class DrydenTurbulence:
    """
    Turbulence as white noise through shaping filters.

        H_u(s) = sigma_u * sqrt(2*L_u/(pi*V)) / (1 + (L_u/V)*s)
        H_v, H_w have an additional zero (second-order forms)

    Implemented as discrete state-space filters, updated at the simulation
    rate. Filter coefficients depend on V, so they are recomputed when
    airspeed changes materially.
    """
    def __init__(self, intensity: str, altitude: float,
                 rng: np.random.Generator): ...
    def step(self, V: float, dt: float) -> np.ndarray:
        """Returns (3,) turbulence velocity in body axes, m/s."""

class WindField:
    """Composes steady wind, turbulence and optional discrete gusts."""
    def velocity_n(self, t: float, pos_n: np.ndarray, V: float,
                   dt: float) -> np.ndarray: ...
```

Intensity levels (light / moderate / severe) set `sigma_w` to roughly 1, 2 and
4 m/s at low altitude, with `sigma_u` and `sigma_v` derived per the standard.

Add a 1-cosine discrete gust for the disturbance-rejection scenario — it gives
you a clean, repeatable "recovery time" metric, which is much easier to interpret
than a statistic over continuous turbulence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Steady wind alone: aircraft flying north into a 10 m/s north wind shows
      `V = 35` m/s while ground speed stays 25. This is the T2.2 test, now with
      a real wind model behind it
- [ ] **Spectrum check.** Run 600 s of turbulence, compute the PSD by Welch's
      method, and confirm it matches the analytic Dryden PSD within a factor of
      2 across the band 0.1–10 rad/s. This is the test that proves you built
      Dryden and not just filtered noise
- [ ] Measured turbulence standard deviation matches the configured `sigma`
      within 10%
- [ ] Reproducible from a seed, bit for bit
- [ ] Zero intensity gives exactly zero turbulence
- [ ] Filter remains stable as `V` varies from 15 to 40 m/s
- [ ] A 1-cosine gust produces the expected peak magnitude and duration

## Notes

The PSD test is worth the effort. Turbulence models are easy to get subtly wrong
— a missing `sqrt`, the wrong length scale — and the result still *looks* like
turbulence on a time plot. Only the spectrum reveals it. Save the PSD comparison
plot; it goes in the report's environment-model section and it is exactly the
kind of evidence that distinguishes a validated model from an assumed one.
